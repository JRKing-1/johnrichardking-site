THE BOOK

This book was not planned.

It emerged over time from lived seasons where obedience carried cost and clarity arrived only after loss. What began as private writing became a record of formation. Not a testimony shaped after the fact, but language written from inside the work itself.

The book is titled A Severe Act of Mercy.

The phrase is drawn directly from Scripture.

"Blessed is the one whom God corrects; so do not despise the discipline of the Almighty. For he wounds, but he also binds up; he injures, but his hands also heal."

Job 5:17–18

The title names a kind of mercy that does not soothe, but saves.
A mercy that closes doors we would have walked through forever.
A mercy that wounds in order to heal.

This book traces a personal journey through rupture, surrender, and rebuilding. It is not written to explain pain away, but to bear witness to how Christ meets a person inside it.

There is no promise of restoration as reward.
There is no formula offered for relief.
What is given instead is an honest account of obedience, loss, endurance, and the slow work of becoming aligned again.

The book is rooted in Scripture and lived experience.
It is written for those who have discovered that faith does not remove suffering, but often requires deeper presence within it.

This is not a book for quick consumption.
It is meant to be read with attention, patience, and openness to being unsettled.

Some readers will recognize their own story in these pages.
Others may not be ready for it yet.

Both responses are honest.

Status

A Severe Act of Mercy is currently being completed.
When it is ready to be released, those who have chosen to stay close to the work will be the first to know.

A Note to the Reader

If you are carrying loss that has not been resolved,
if obedience has cost you more than you expected,
or if faith has brought you into questions rather than answers,
this book may feel familiar.

There is no requirement to read it.
Only an offering, made carefully, when the time comes.

John Richard King
