    • A Story Still Being Written

    The journey of an author learning to witness the work of God in unexpected places.

    John Richard King is an author and speaker dedicated to exploring the intersection of faith, loss, and spiritual transformation. His work focuses on the difficult and beautiful reality that God often meets us not in our moments of triumph, but in our seasons of uncertainty and pain.

    For the past fifteen years, John has been involved in spiritual formation work, guiding individuals and communities through seasons of change and challenge. He has served in various roles—as a spiritual director, retreat leader, and pastoral counselor—always with the conviction that our stories matter deeply and that God's grace is especially evident in the chapters we would rather skip.

    John's own journey toward this calling was not straightforward. After years of pursuing what he thought was his calling, John experienced a significant loss that forced him to reconsider everything. In the darkness of that season, he discovered something unexpected: God's mercy often comes in forms we don't recognize, and faith is not about having all the answers, but about learning to live well with difficult questions.

    That experience became the genesis of his writing and speaking. He felt called to help others navigate the terrain between what they planned and what God is asking of them. He believes that there is a particular grace available in loss, and that the work of God in our lives often looks nothing like we imagined it would.

    His Work

    John's writing explores themes of faith, loss, mercy, restraint, obedience, and spiritual formation. His essays and reflections are published in various journals and platforms, and he is currently working on his memoir, A Severe Act of Mercy, which tells the story of his own journey through loss toward deeper faith.

    In addition to his writing, John leads retreats and speaks at churches and conferences, inviting audiences to reflect on how God's work in their lives might be inviting them toward transformation they did not anticipate. His speaking is marked by vulnerability, theological depth, and a pastoral concern for his listeners' spiritual well-being.

    Personal Life

    When not writing or speaking, John spends time with his family, exploring questions through reading in theology and literature, and walking in nature. He is an avid reader of both contemporary and classic spiritual writers, and he believes that literature and theology have much to teach us about faith, doubt, and the work of God in human hearts.

    John lives with his family and is a member of a local faith community where he continues to learn what it means to witness the work of God in the lives of those around him—not as an expert, but as a fellow traveler learning to trust in God's severe and tender mercy.
